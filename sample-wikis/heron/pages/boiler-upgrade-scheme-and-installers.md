# Boiler Upgrade Scheme and Choosing an MCS Installer

> Current Boiler Upgrade Scheme grant levels and rules for England and Wales, how the application works, the budget picture, and what to check before signing with an installer.

## Grant levels (from 21 July 2026)

From gov.uk, one grant per property:

| Technology | Grant |
| --- | --- |
| Air source heat pump (air-to-water) | £7,500 |
| Ground or water source heat pump | £7,500 |
| Air-to-air heat pump | £2,500 |
| Biomass boiler (rural, off-gas only) | £5,000 |

Off the gas grid and replacing oil or LPG, the air-to-water and ground source grants rise to **£9,000** until March 2027 (an extra £1,500). The government notice setting these values took effect 21 July 2026.

For a Victorian terrace in a town, the relevant number is almost certainly **£7,500** for an air source heat pump.

## Who qualifies

- You must **own** the property. Second homes, rentals and business premises count.
- You must be **replacing fossil fuel heating**: gas, oil, LPG or direct electric.
- Previous insulation funding does not disqualify you.
- Not eligible: most new builds still being built, social housing, properties that already had government money for a heat pump or biomass boiler, hybrid systems (heat pump plus gas boiler), and replacing an existing low carbon system.
- Capacity limit: 45 kWth for a single system. A terrace will be far below this.
- England and Wales only. Scotland has its own schemes.

## How the application works

1. Get quotes from MCS certified installers.
2. The installer confirms eligibility.
3. You agree a quote. The installer applies to Ofgem on your behalf.
4. The grant comes off your bill. It should appear on the quote and the invoice.
5. Ofgem contacts you to confirm the installer is acting for you, and may phone or visit. MCS may also inspect.

Timing rule: the installer must commission the heat pump within **120 days** of applying, or the grant is lost. Do not let an installer apply before they can schedule the work.

## Budget picture

Trade press (Selectra, updated 6 November 2026) reports the scheme budget is held at £400m for 2026-27 and 2027-28, below the £600m previously planned for 2027-28, and estimates this year's money could run out in early 2027. That last point is their projection, not an official date. If you want the grant this financial year, do not leave quotes until spring.

## Choosing an installer

**Baseline checks:**

- **MCS certified for heat pumps.** Use the MCS Find an Installer tool. It also shows whether the business is registered for the Boiler Upgrade Scheme. MCS certification is required for the grant.
- **Consumer code membership.** The Energy Saving Trust notes MCS members must belong to a consumer code approved under Trading Standards, such as RECC or HIES. These require deposit protection, a 7-day cooling-off period and a workmanship warranty of at least two years.
- **TrustMark** registration is another check the Energy Saving Trust suggests.

**Quality signals beyond the badge:**

- A **room-by-room heat loss** report, with sensible assumptions. See [[heat-loss-and-sizing]].
- A **design flow temperature** stated in writing, ideally 45°C or below, with a radiator schedule showing which radiators change. See [[radiators-and-flow-temperature]].
- A **performance estimate** (seasonal efficiency at that flow temperature) and an annual running cost estimate you can check against [[running-costs-vs-gas]].
- A **noise and siting plan**. In England, since 29 May 2025, permitted development allows units within 1 metre of a boundary, up to two units, and larger outdoor units (up to 1.5 m³), but the MCS 020 noise calculation still applies. Terraces with small yards make this a real constraint.
- **Aftercare**: who commissions, who tunes the controls in the first winter, and what the warranty covers.

**Red flags:** a quote with no heat loss figures, a "same size as your boiler" recommendation, pressure to sign the same day, or an installer keen to submit the grant application before agreeing dates.
