# The Oil Price Cap, Attestations and Insurance

> How the G7/EU price cap on Russian seaborne oil works, how its level moved from $60 to $47.60 to $44.10, the attestation regime, and why insurance defines the shadow fleet.

Research notes from public sources, as of November 2026.

## What the cap does

The cap does not ban Russian oil. It controls Western services. EU operators may provide maritime transport and related services, such as insurance, finance and shipping, for Russian crude and products only if the oil is sold at or below the cap. A tanker carrying oil above the cap must do without those services. This is why definitions of a shadow tanker focus on G7+ links (see [[shadow-fleet-size-estimates]]).

## Cap levels and dates

| Date | Crude cap | Who | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 Dec 2022 | $60/bbl | G7 and Australia | Time-limited exception for oil loaded before that date |
| 5 Feb 2023 | Products: $100 premium-to-crude, $45 discount-to-crude | G7 and partners | Diesel, kerosene, gasoline vs fuel oil, naphtha |
| 3 Sep 2025 | $47.60/bbl | EU (18th package, adopted 18 Jul 2025) | First "dynamic" cap |
| 2 Sep 2025, 23:01 BST | $47.60/bbl | UK | 45-day wind-down to 17 Oct 2025 |
| 1 Feb 2026 | $44.10/bbl | EU and UK | First automatic review, 15 Jan 2026; UK from 23:01 on 31 Jan 2026 |
| 23 Jul 2026 | Held at $44.10 | EU (21st package) | Automatic adjustment paused until 15 Jul 2027, interim review |

Australia, Japan and New Zealand joined the lower EU cap by October 2025. The US cap was still $60 at that point.

## The dynamic mechanism

The EU's 18th package added an automatic formula. The cap is set at 15% below the average market price of Urals crude over the previous 22 weeks. The Commission reviews it every six months. Contracts under an old cap get a wind-down: 90 days in the EU in January 2026, and offloading by 16 April 2026 in the UK.

The formula means the cap would rise with oil prices. The 21st package pause stopped that. Urals averaged $90.70 in September 2026, more than double the $44.10 cap, according to CREA.

## The attestation regime

Service providers often cannot see the oil price, so the coalition built a safe harbour based on documents. OFAC's revised guidance of 20 December 2023 sorts actors into three tiers:

- **Tier 1, direct price access** (traders, commodity brokers): keep documents that show the oil was bought at or below the cap, including itemised ancillary costs.
- **Tier 2, sometimes able to get prices** (trade finance banks, customs brokers, shipping agents): request and keep the same documents, or get attestations where that is not practical.
- **Tier 3, no price access** (insurers, P&I clubs, shipowners, flag registries): get and keep attestations that the oil was bought at or below the cap.

US providers were expected to comply by 19 February 2024. Reasonable reliance on these records gives a safe harbour, unless the provider knew or had reason to know they were false.

## Insurance is the dividing line

KSE treats a tanker as shadow only if it has no cover from the International Group (IG) of P&I Clubs. CREA counts tankers not owned or insured in G7+ countries. P&I cover is the third-party liability insurance that pays for things like spills and wreck removal. What replaced it:

- **Russian insurers.** In June 2026, KSE counted 48 tankers with Russian insurance in the shadow fleet. 42 of them (88%) were sanctioned by at least one jurisdiction. On 10 January 2025 the US designated two Russian marine insurers, Ingosstrakh and Alfastrakhovanie.
- **Reinsurance ban.** The EU's 19th package (October 2025) banned reinsurance of shadow fleet vessels.

## The next step: a full services ban

The EU's 20th package (23 April 2026) created the legal basis for a full maritime services ban on Russian crude and products, pending coordinated G7 action. After the 21st package, West P&I reported that "no decision on activating it has been taken yet."
