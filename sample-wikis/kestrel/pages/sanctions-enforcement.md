# Enforcement: Vessel Designations, Inspections and Boardings

> What the EU, UK and US have actually done to the shadow fleet since 2024: package-by-package vessel listings, the big US actions, Danish straits checks, boardings at sea, and which outcomes analysts can measure.

Research notes from public sources, as of November 2026.

## Two kinds of enforcement

1. **Designations.** Governments list specific vessels. In the EU, a listed ship is banned from EU ports and from a broad range of maritime services, including insurance. US designations block property and expose counterparties to secondary sanctions risk.
2. **Physical action.** States inspect, detain or board ships in their waters, usually on safety, documentation or flag grounds.

## EU vessel listings by package

| Package | Adopted | Vessels added | Total listed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 17th | May 2025 | n/a | 342 |
| 18th | 18 Jul 2025 | 105 | n/a |
| 19th | 23 Oct 2025 | 117 | 557 |
| 20th | 23 Apr 2026 | 46 | 632 |
| 21st | 23 Jul 2026 | 41 | over 670 |

The 17th package listed the tanker Eagle S, according to the Finnish government. The 19th package also banned reinsurance of shadow fleet vessels and listed registries that the EU said provided false flags. The 21st package listed five bunkering tankers on the stated ground that they regularly refuelled already designated vessels. West P&I described this as the first use of widened criteria covering vessels that provide support services to the shadow fleet. The package also designated a crewing agency, which Hill Dickinson described as a first.

## UK designations

- 21 July 2025: 135 tankers designated.
- 7 January 2026: 520 shadow fleet vessels sanctioned to date.
- 25 March 2026: 544 vessels. The same day, the UK said its armed forces and law enforcement could now interdict UK-sanctioned vessels transiting UK waters.
- 8 October 2026: 12 more tankers, taking the total to "over 600".

## US actions

- **10 January 2025.** OFAC designated 183 vessels, including 143 tankers, plus the producers Gazprom Neft and Surgutneftegas and the marine insurers Ingosstrakh and Alfastrakhovanie.
- **22 October 2025.** OFAC designated Rosneft and Lukoil and dozens of subsidiaries. General License 126, for winding down transactions, expired on 21 November 2025. Treasury warned non-US persons, including banks, of secondary sanctions risk.
- **7 January 2026.** US forces intercepted the tanker Bella 1 between the UK and Iceland, with UK support. The US had sanctioned the vessel under its counter-Iran program.

## Inspections and boardings in Europe

- **Denmark.** In December 2024 Denmark agreed to check the insurance of passing tankers. In February 2025 it said the Danish Maritime Authority would carry out port state control checks on ships anchored off Skagen that "cannot be considered in innocent passage", with tankers prioritised.
- **France.** In late September 2025 French forces boarded the tanker Boracay, at anchor off Saint-Nazaire. The ship is listed under EU and UK sanctions. The Brest prosecutor opened an investigation into failure to prove the vessel's nationality and failure to comply with orders. On 30 March 2026 a French court convicted the captain in absentia of failing to comply with the boarding order and sentenced him to one year in prison and a fine. His lawyer said he would appeal.
- **Finland.** Finland seized the Eagle S after the December 2024 cable damage. The criminal case is covered in [[safety-and-environmental-risk]].

## What can be measured

Analysts cite a few indicators. They do not prove cause and effect.

- **Sanctioned share is high.** In June 2026, 73% of active Russian shadow tankers had sailed while under EU sanctions (KSE). In September 2026, sanctioned shadow tankers carried 67% of Russian crude (CREA). Listing has not stopped those ships from trading.
- **False flags fell.** Falsely flagged shadow tankers fell from 120 to 48 between September 2025 and September 2026, while Russian-flagged shadow tankers rose from 22 to 50 (CREA). See [[how-the-shadow-fleet-operates]].
- **Discounts widened in the weeks after the US oil major sanctions.** The Urals FOB discount to Brent neared $20/bbl in November 2025, the widest in a year (Quantum Commodity Intelligence). See [[market-effects-and-buyers]].
- **Revenue.** In January 2026 the UK government said Russian oil revenues were down 27% compared with October 2024.

## Open questions

- The EU's full maritime services ban has a legal basis but has not been activated (see [[price-cap-and-insurance]]).
- Jurisdiction at sea is contested. In the Eagle S case, a Finnish district court and the appeals court reached opposite answers on whether Finland could try the crew (see [[safety-and-environmental-risk]]).
