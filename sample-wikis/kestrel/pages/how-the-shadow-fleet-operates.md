# How the Shadow Fleet Operates: Transfers, AIS Gaps and Flags

> The operating playbook as described by regulators and trackers: ship-to-ship transfers, switching off or spoofing AIS, false and fraudulent flags, opaque ownership, and how those patterns shifted in 2025 and 2026.

Research notes from public sources, as of November 2026.

## The basic idea

A shadow tanker moves oil without the Western services that the price cap controls: G7+ ownership, management, flags and P&I insurance (see [[price-cap-and-insurance]]). The techniques below make cargoes harder to trace, or ships harder to hold to account. None is illegal in every setting. Declared, safely run ship-to-ship transfers are normal industry practice.

## Ship-to-ship transfers

In a ship-to-ship (STS) transfer, cargo moves between two tankers at sea. It can mix cargo origins, change the paperwork trail, or move oil from a smaller vessel onto a larger one for a long voyage.

The IMO Assembly adopted a resolution on the "dark fleet" on 6 December 2023. It calls on flag states to apply measures that lawfully prohibit or regulate STS transfers, and on ships to update their STS operation plans, especially for mid-ocean transfers. Port states should notify flag states when ships deliberately avoid detection, and should monitor STS operations in their territorial seas and exclusive economic zones.

KSE's August 2026 Russia Oil Tracker counted 154 loaded shadow tankers in July 2026 that took part in STS transfers or left Russian ports.

## AIS gaps and spoofing

AIS is the transponder system that broadcasts a ship's identity and position. The IMO resolution names "switching off" tracking transponders as a sign of deliberate evasion and recommends enhanced port state inspections for those ships. Trackers also describe spoofing, where a ship broadcasts a false position or a false identity.

## False flags and fraudulent registries

A ship needs a flag state. Shadow tankers have moved to small open registries, and in some cases to registries that do not exist.

- **IMO count.** Ahead of its Legal Sub-Committee in early 2026, the IMO identified 529 vessels operating under false flags. 356 of them were not classed by any classification society. Examples include 83 ships recorded as falsely using the Comoros flag, 74 using Guyana's, and 13 tankers linked to false use of Tonga's flag, whose registry closed in 2002. Gambia removed 72 ships after it found forged certificates.
- **EU action.** The EU's 19th package (23 October 2025) listed maritime registries that it said provided false flags to shadow fleet ships.
- **The 2026 shift to the Russian flag.** CREA counted 120 falsely flagged shadow tankers in September 2025 and 48 at the end of September 2026. Over the same period, Russian-flagged shadow tankers delivering Russian oil rose from 22 to 50. By September 2026 they carried 36% of shadow fleet oil, while false flags carried 2%. Cameroon-flagged tankers in the Russian trade fell from 140 in February 2026 to 69. The shift came in the same period as more boardings and inspections of stateless or falsely flagged ships by European states (see [[sanctions-enforcement]]).

## Opaque ownership and management

Lloyd's List Intelligence describes a network of "brass plate companies and middlemen" beyond Western reach, with unclear ownership, little inspection history and no insurance. KSE tracks who manages the ships. In June 2026, Chinese managers handled 34% of shadow fleet volume and Russian managers 22%. The top 10 managers handled 29%, and former Sovcomflot operators handled 17%.

## Age and condition

Shadow tankers are old. KSE found that tankers older than 15 years made up 94% of the shadow fleet in June 2026. Old ships cost little to buy, but they carry more risk (see [[safety-and-environmental-risk]]).

## Route geography

Russian Baltic exports pass through the Danish straits and the English Channel. CREA reported no falsely flagged shadow tankers loading at Russia's Baltic or Arctic ports since May 2026. Denmark began port state control checks at the Skagen anchorage in 2025, on the same route.
