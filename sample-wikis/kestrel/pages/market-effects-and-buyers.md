# Market Effects: Urals Discounts, Freight Rates and Buyers

> What the shadow fleet and the sanctions against it have done to the Urals discount, tanker freight rates and Russian export revenue, and who buys the oil: India, China and Turkey.

Research notes from public sources, as of November 2026.

## The Urals discount

Analysts watch the discount of Russian Urals crude to Brent as a gauge of sanctions pressure. A wider discount means Russia earns less per barrel than the benchmark.

- **November 2025.** Three weeks after the US designated Rosneft and Lukoil on 22 October 2025, the Urals FOB discount to Brent neared $20/bbl, the widest in a year, according to Quantum Commodity Intelligence (12 November 2025).
- **July 2026.** KSE put the average Urals FOB price at about $57/bbl, down about $4 from June. The discount to Dated Brent widened by $2 in the month.
- **August and September 2026.** CREA put the discount at $23/bbl, or 21%, in both months. Urals averaged $90.70 in September 2026, up 32% from August.

The large price swing in 2026 matters for the cap. At $90.70, Urals traded at more than double the $44.10 EU/UK cap. The EU paused its automatic cap formula in July 2026, so the cap did not rise with the market (see [[price-cap-and-insurance]]).

## Who buys

CREA tracks buyers of Russian crude from December 2022, when the EU embargo and the cap began, to the end of September 2026:

| Buyer | Share of Russian crude exports | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| China | 50% | also 13% of Russian oil product exports |
| India | 37% | crude was 88% of India's Russian fossil fuel purchases in Sep 2026 |
| Turkiye | 5% | also 26% of Russian oil product exports |

KSE gives a monthly view for July 2026:

- **India:** 2.6 million b/d of Russian crude, 54% of India's total crude imports, matching June's record.
- **China:** up 24% month on month to 1.5 million b/d, 21% of China's crude imports.
- **Turkey:** 138 kb/d, 28% of its crude imports.

Shares from different trackers do not match exactly. CREA's are cumulative over almost four years. KSE's are for one month and are measured against each buyer's total imports.

## Revenue

KSE estimated Russian oil export revenue at $13.8 billion in July 2026, down $2.0 billion from June. Its base case was $182 billion for all of 2026. In January 2026 the UK government said Russia's oil revenues were down 27% compared with October 2024.

## Freight rates

Sanctions take ships out of the mainstream fleet, which tightens supply for everyone else.

- **Late 2025.** Reuters reported VLCC rates of about $130,000 per day in recent weeks (December 2025). Jefferies expected VLCC fleet utilisation to rise to 92% in 2026 from 89.5% in 2025. Frontline's CEO said nearly 18% of VLCCs had been hit with sanctions.
- **Early 2026.** Kpler reported that Aframax rates rose to multi-year highs in January 2026. Shadow fleet VLCC employment collapsed that month, with many ships in floating storage off Asia. Kpler linked this to Venezuela's "effective removal from sanctioned trade networks". It also said the VLCC fleet available for unsanctioned trade had been shrinking since 2023, back to 2021 levels.
- **Supply side.** Gibson's head of research said scheduled tanker deliveries in 2026 were the highest since 2009, which adds mainstream capacity.

## Reading the signals together

- A wider discount with steady volumes means Russia is still selling but earns less per barrel.
- Designations move ships between the mainstream and sanctioned pools, so tanker rates respond to enforcement news as well as to oil demand (see [[sanctions-enforcement]]).
- Volumes, prices and buyer shares should be read with the source and month attached, because the trackers measure different things. Fleet size and share estimates are in [[shadow-fleet-size-estimates]].
