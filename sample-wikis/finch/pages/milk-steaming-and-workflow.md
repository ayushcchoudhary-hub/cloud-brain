# Milk Steaming and Workflow

> How to steam silky milk for flat whites and lattes at home, which temperatures to aim for, and a daily workflow that keeps shots consistent and the machine clean.

## What you are aiming for

Good steamed milk is glossy with very fine bubbles, often called microfoam. It should pour like wet paint, not sit on top like stiff froth. The two phases are:

1. **Stretching**: the steam tip sits just under the surface and pulls in air. You hear a light tearing or hissing sound. This is where volume comes from.
2. **Texturing**: the tip goes slightly deeper and the milk spins in a whirlpool. This breaks large bubbles into small ones and mixes the foam through the milk.

## Amounts and timing

- Fill the pitcher to just below the bottom of the spout. Too little milk heats before you can texture it. Too much leaves no room to expand.
- Stretch for a short time only, roughly the first third of steaming. For a flat white, aim for about 10 to 20 percent volume gain. For a latte, a bit more. For a cappuccino, more again.
- Stop at around 55 to 65 C. Above about 70 C, milk starts to taste cooked and the foam breaks down. A good rule: when the pitcher is too hot to hold for more than a second or two, stop.

## Milk choice

- Cold milk from the fridge gives you more time to texture.
- Whole milk is the most forgiving. Skim milk foams easily but the foam is dry and fades fast.
- Barista versions of oat and other plant milks are formulated to foam. Regular versions often split or stay flat.

## Pitcher and steam wand

- A 350 ml pitcher suits one drink. A 600 ml pitcher suits two or larger drinks.
- Single-boiler machines have weak, short steam. Steam less milk at once and expect slower results.
- Purge the wand before and after steaming. Wipe it straight away with a damp cloth so milk does not bake on.

## A daily workflow

1. Turn the machine on early. Many machines need 15 to 30 minutes for the group to fully heat, even when the light says ready.
2. Flush the group for a few seconds to stabilize temperature.
3. Weigh beans, grind, distribute, tamp. See [[espresso-dialing-in]].
4. Pull the shot into a warmed cup on a scale.
5. Steam milk right after, while the shot is fresh.
6. Purge and wipe the wand, flush the group, knock out the puck.

## Weekly and monthly care

- **Weekly**: backflush with water on machines that support it, and with detergent about once a week if you make several drinks a day. Soak the portafilter and basket.
- **Monthly or so**: check and clean the shower screen and gasket area.
- **As needed**: descale on the maker's schedule. Water choice changes how often, see [[water-for-espresso]].
- **Every year or two**: replace the group gasket if shots start leaking around the portafilter.

## Common problems

- **Big bubbles**: too much stretching or the tip too high for too long.
- **No foam**: tip too deep from the start, or the milk is already warm.
- **Screaming sound**: tip too deep with no air intake. Lift slightly.
- **Foam separates in the cup**: texturing too short. Spin longer and swirl the pitcher before pouring.
